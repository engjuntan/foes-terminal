# Juta Success — the concrete that isn't

A proposal, not a ruling. Two linked mysteries in Bandawang, built out of
things the vault already says, plus the one place they contradict each
other and three ways to resolve it.

---

## The contradiction, first

The GM's direction: *"make it a running concrete plant, using the
existing rusted concrete plant we already have… the underlying mystery is
that the plant isn't actually working — it's just the latest Axe Gang
scheme."*

`Locations/Bandawang/The Cement Works.md` says the opposite, and says it
load-bearingly:

> It has not run since the war… Nobody owns it. The Works sits between
> North Bandawang, Bandawang Lama and Bandawang Baru without belonging to
> any of them… there is nothing left in it worth carrying out, **which is
> exactly why it stays neutral: there is nothing here to fight over**.

The emptiness is the neutrality. A working plant on that site is a prize,
and a prize cannot be neutral ground. The Council meeting "at the gates
on nobody's turf" stops making sense the moment the gates are worth
something.

**Three ways out.**

| | What it costs | What it buys |
|---|---|---|
| **A. The Works stays dead; Juta operates a smaller plant elsewhere** | A new location to write and draw | Canon untouched. But it throws away the best thing about the idea — the scheme is much better if it is hiding in the one place nobody is allowed to own. |
| **B. The Works stays dead, and the scheme is to make it LOOK alive** (recommended) | Nothing. Every existing line stays true. | The plant genuinely does not work. The Axe Gang have not reopened it; they have dressed one corner of it. Neutrality is not broken — it is the *cover*, because neutral ground is the one place in Bandawang nobody inspects. |
| **C. Rewrite the Works as operational** | The neutral-ground ruling, the Council-at-the-gates detail, the "dead heart" framing, and the entrance art showing chained gates and overgrowth | A straightforwardly running factory, which is the least interesting of the three. |

The rest of this document assumes **B**.

## What is actually happening

Bandawang Baru is being built. The art shows it: poured concrete going up
against and onto pre-war timber houses, scaffolding, cement bags on the
street. Somebody is supplying that concrete, in quantity, and has been
for a while.

**Juta Success supplies it.** They present as a going concern operating
out of the Cement Works — a yard inside the fence line, a weighbridge, a
mixer that runs loudly at the right times of day, men in a uniform, paper
with a letterhead. It is dysfunctional and old and nobody expects
otherwise; everything in Bandawang is dysfunctional and old.

**The plant has not made a gram of cement.** There is no kiln running.
What Juta Success sells is the Works itself: rubble broken out of its own
floors, walls and silo linings, crushed, screened, bagged, and sold back
to the city as new product. The note already says the builders quarry the
Works for aggregate. Juta Success has industrialised that and put a price
on it.

**It is an Axe Gang operation.** Not a front for laundering — a product
scam. The gang controls North Bandawang and the Scrapyard, and crushing
rubble is exactly the plant they already have. What they lacked was a
reason anyone would pay well for it, and a face respectable enough to
sell it.

**Ishtar is the face.** A Protectorate-raised conman with no interest in
engineering, who sells things he does not own and brokers between people
who should not be in a room together. He is already drawn mid-handshake
at the half-built forecourt of Syurga. He does not own Juta Success and
is not in the Axe Gang; he takes a cut per contract and keeps the paper
clean enough that a buyer feels foolish asking.

**The concrete is bad.** Not uniformly — that is what makes it dangerous.
170-year-old concrete rubble as aggregate carries old chlorides and dead
cement that will not re-bind. Some batches are nearly fine. Some set and
hold for a year. Some never reach strength at all and fail without
warning, usually under load, usually at night when the day's heat leaves
the slab.

## The two quests

These are deliberately separable. A table that only wants one can take
the first and never learn the second exists.

### Quest 1 — "The Buildings Are Cracking" (the visible one)

**Hook.** Something in Bandawang Baru fails. A balcony drops, a column
spalls, a new wall opens a crack a hand wide overnight. Nobody died this
time.

**What the players can do.** Walk the district and compare. The failures
are not random — they cluster by *batch*, not by builder or by age. A
Science or Repair check on a broken edge finds the aggregate is visibly
old concrete, not stone: rounded, grey, with the ghost of a different mix
inside it.

**Where it leads.** Delivery notes lead to Juta Success. Juta Success
leads to a yard inside the Cement Works that is busy in the morning and
empty by two. Ishtar's name is on the contracts.

**Resolution options.** Expose it publicly (and face a city that now has
no concrete supply at all). Extort it. Take it over. Force Juta to blend
proper limestone, which means reopening the quarry in the hills, which
means dealing with North Bandawang.

### Quest 2 — "The Plant Doesn't Work" (the hidden one)

**Hook.** Only reachable from inside Quest 1. A worker says something
that does not fit: they have never seen the kiln lit. Or the players
notice the mixer runs at 9am and 4pm and never otherwise, which is when
buyers visit.

**The reveal.** There is no production. The entire operation is a crusher
and a bagging line fed by the building it stands in. Juta Success is
slowly eating the Cement Works and selling it to Bandawang Baru by the
tonne.

**Why it matters beyond the scam.** The Works is structural. It is the
city's neutral ground and the physical fact that keeps three districts
from each other's throats. The Axe Gang are quarrying away the one thing
nobody is allowed to own, and when enough of it is gone there is no
"nobody's turf" left to meet on.

**That is the real stake**, and it is political rather than criminal. A
party that only wants money can take a cut and walk. A party that
understands what the Works is for has a harder decision.

## The Kai thread

`Taman Hidup Wataniah Report.md` already has Chang writing:

> **Kai** – CEO of Juta Success. The contractors hired to 'fix' this
> town. Apparently they were using proper concrete. Who cares. He's
> missing. Always a good sign.

Same company, two towns, and the CEO vanished between them. Juta Success
did Taman Hidup first, stretched the mix there too, and Kai disappeared
when it started to show. Whoever runs the Bandawang operation now either
replaced him or removed him.

This costs nothing to plant and ties the module to the main campaign
without requiring anyone to have played it. A party that never touches
New Federation Blues just finds a company with a missing CEO and a
pattern. A party that played it already knows the name.

## What makes sense, and what does not

**Works well:**

- The scam is already half-written. "Builders quarry the Works itself for
  aggregate" is in the note; this only asks who organised it.
- It is physical and testable. Players can break a piece of wall and look
  at it. No lore dump required.
- The Axe Gang's existing holdings — North Bandawang, the Scrapyard — are
  exactly the infrastructure a rubble-crushing operation needs.
- Ishtar gets something to do that is in character and does not require
  his secrets.
- Bandawang Baru's "new concrete fused onto old timber" look, which is
  already drawn, becomes a clue rather than set dressing.

**Does not work, or needs a decision:**

- **It cannot run forever.** Eating a building to sell it is a finite
  business, and a sharp player will ask what happens when the Works runs
  out. That is either a feature (the scheme has a deadline, and the Axe
  Gang know it) or a hole. Recommend treating it as a deadline.
- **Why has nobody noticed?** A yard inside the Works is not subtle. The
  answer has to be that neutral ground is nobody's job to police — the
  Enforcers have no jurisdiction there, and each district assumes another
  is watching. Worth stating explicitly in the note or it reads as an
  oversight.
- **Ishtar's involvement risks his other threads.** He is already a
  double-and-triple agent with a hidden benefactor. Adding a concrete
  scam is fine, but it should be beneath his real interests — a reliable
  earner he barely thinks about — not his main plot. Otherwise the reveal
  of the bigger thing lands softer.
- **The Caliphate and Federation angle is a trap.** The log had Kai
  funnelling profits to Federation military coffers in exchange for a
  platoon. That is a third faction in a story that works better as a
  local crime. Recommend leaving it out of Bandawang and keeping it in
  Taman Hidup if it is wanted at all.
- **Bad concrete should not have killed anyone yet.** If it already has,
  the city would have acted. The tension is that it is *about* to, and
  the players are the first people to see the pattern.
